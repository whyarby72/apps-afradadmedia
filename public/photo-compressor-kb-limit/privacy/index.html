1. Scope

This Privacy Policy describes how Photo Compressor: KB Limit handles information when you use the Android app. The app's core purpose is to help you reduce a JPEG/JPG toward a file-size limit you provide, verify the resulting size, and save or share a copy.

The policy separates the app's local photo-processing behavior from data that may be processed by Google advertising and consent services when those services are enabled in the release you are using.

2. Photos you choose

When you choose a JPEG/JPG, the app may read information needed for the compression job, including the file's display name, MIME type, byte size, image dimensions and EXIF orientation when available.

The selected photo is processed locally on your device for the core compression workflow. The app's core compression code does not upload the selected source JPEG to an Afradad Media server for compression.

The app code is not designed to intentionally send your source JPEG bytes or the KB/MB target you enter to Google Mobile Ads.

3. Temporary files, Save and Share

Temporary result

A generated result is temporarily stored in the app's cache so it can be previewed, saved or shared. The result cache is reused as new outputs are produced, and Android may clear app cache as part of normal system storage management.

Save

Saving is an explicit action. When you choose Save, the app writes a separate JPEG copy to Pictures/Reduce Photo Size. Saved copies remain under your device and storage management until you delete or otherwise remove them.

Share

Sharing is also an explicit action. The app provides the result through a read-only Android content URI to the app or service you choose. After you share a file, the receiving service's own privacy practices apply.

Original photo

The normal compression workflow does not overwrite the source photo.

4. Image metadata

When the app recompresses an image, the new JPEG is produced from decoded image pixels, so source metadata is not preserved in the same way as the original file. The app uses EXIF orientation information, when available, to avoid silent orientation errors during processing.

If a source image already meets the requested limit and does not need recompression, the app may copy that source instead. Metadata behavior can therefore differ between a recompressed result and an already-ready copied result.

5. Accounts and developer cloud storage

No user account or login is required for the core compression workflow. Afradad Media does not require a developer-owned cloud photo store or backend server to perform the core compression job.

6. Advertising and Google consent services

When advertising is enabled in the app release you are using, the app may use the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) to display a banner advertisement and Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) to request or manage consent choices where required.

Google advertising and consent services may process device, network, advertising, interaction, diagnostic, or fraud-prevention related information according to Google's current SDK behavior, configuration, consent state, and policies.

This third-party processing is separate from the JPEG content handled by the app's local compression workflow. The app code does not intentionally send your selected JPEG bytes or the KB/MB target you enter to Google Mobile Ads.

The current product architecture does not use Firebase Analytics, custom analytics, or ad mediation. Advertising or consent failures are not intended to block the core photo-compression workflow.

7. Consent and privacy choices

The current monetization/privacy architecture uses Google's UMP consent information as a gate before ad requests are permitted. Where Google reports that privacy options are required, the app is designed to expose a Privacy choices entry point.

The exact consent experience can vary by region, applicable requirements, Google configuration and the release artifact in use.

8. Analytics

The current product architecture does not use Firebase Analytics or a custom analytics service. Google Mobile Ads is a separate third-party advertising SDK and may process information according to Google's own data practices.

9. Permissions and system access

The app uses Android system photo/document and media APIs to let you choose, save, and share files.

SDK dependencies may add permissions or components through Android's merged manifest. This policy therefore does not present a fixed, exhaustive permission list based only on the app's handwritten manifest.

10. Retention and deletion

A temporary compressed result is kept in app cache for the app's result workflow and may be replaced by later results or cleared by Android as part of normal cache management.

Copies you explicitly save remain in your device media storage until you delete or remove them. Files you explicitly share are then handled by the destination app or service you selected.

The core compression workflow does not create a developer-owned server copy of your selected photo for processing.

11. Security

The core compression job is designed to operate locally on the device, reducing the need to transmit your selected photo to an Afradad Media server for processing. No software or transmission method can be described as perfectly secure, and third-party services used by an ad-enabled release are governed by their own security practices.

12. Your choices

You decide which JPEG/JPG to select, whether to save a compressed result, and whether and where to share it. Where Google requires or exposes privacy choices through UMP, the app is designed to make the applicable privacy-options entry point available.

13. Children's privacy and target audience

Photo Compressor: KB Limit is intended for an adult audience aged 18 and over. It is not designed or directed to children.

If the target audience, app design, advertising configuration, or store declarations change in a future release, this section and the related advertising/privacy controls must be reviewed again before that change is published.

14. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated when the app's features, advertising configuration, SDKs, legal requirements or data practices change. Material changes should be reflected on this page with an updated effective date.
